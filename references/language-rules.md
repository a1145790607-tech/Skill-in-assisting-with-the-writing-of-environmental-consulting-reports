# Language Rules

## 1. Prefer Direct Positive Expression

Avoid explanatory turns such as:

- "not ... but ...";
- "it should not be understood as ...";
- "from a certain perspective";
- repeated explanation of why the report is not an approval document.
- "this section mainly..." at the beginning of every subsection;
- "this provides a basis for subsequent..." at the end of every subsection;
- repeated "as stated above" or "as analyzed below" when the paragraph already follows the report order.

Preferred:

> This report serves as technical support material and focuses on impact range, risk conditions and management requirements after project implementation.

Better still, state the positive scope directly:

> This report focuses on impact range, risk conditions and operation-period management requirements after project implementation.

Use boundary statements only when a reader may reasonably misunderstand the scope. When a boundary is needed, write it as a neutral scope statement instead of "not B, but A".

Weak:

> This section does not judge whether the project is approvable, but only analyzes the water-environment response.

Preferred:

> This section analyzes the water-environment response under the project operation scenario, focusing on concentration change, affected area and control-section variation.

## 2. Replace Vague Praise With Technical Content

Use vague praise only when immediately followed by object, evidence, and condition.

Weak words:

- important significance;
- strong support;
- scientific and reasonable;
- feasible;
- significantly improved;
- systematic and comprehensive;
- further strengthen;
- continuously improve;
- overall controllable;
- limited impact.
- significant;
- obvious;
- acceptable;
- reasonable.

Preferred pattern:

> The study clarifies concentration changes at sensitive sections, main risk scenarios and monitoring priorities after project implementation, which can support operation scheduling and follow-up monitoring plan preparation.

Before using these judgement words, check whether the text has a standard, baseline, scenario comparison, control section, comparable case, or explicit assumption. If not, replace the judgement with a factual description.

## 3. Avoid Routine Opening And Closing Summaries

Do not add a stock opening and closing to every chapter or subsection. It creates repeated, circular prose and weakens the technical point.

Avoid routine openings:

> This section is based on the previous chapter and analyzes...

> In order to provide a basis for subsequent analysis, this section...

Avoid routine closings:

> In summary, the above analysis provides a basis for the following chapter.

> Through the above analysis, this section clarifies...

Use a short closure only when the section has a decision-relevant answer, a scenario comparison result, a key parameter for later modeling, or a management condition that must be carried forward.

If the analysis already states that answer, do not repeat it in a summary. Keep an incidental explanation to a brief supported clause or sentence; do not turn it into a new topic merely to make the prose appear complete. Figure details, general mechanisms, and generic management advice must not crowd out the subsection's actual question.

Preferred:

> The dry-season simulation shows that the concentration increase is concentrated near the inlet area, while the lake-center and outlet control sections remain close to the background scenario.

## 4. State Supported Conclusions Directly

Use the strongest specific judgement supported by the evidence, with its material scope stated once. Do not weaken every conclusion with “一定”, “可能”, or “可为……提供支持”, and do not replace those words mechanically when uncertainty is real.

Weak, when the evidence already establishes stability:

> 可为丰水期基准预测的稳健性提供支持。

Preferred:

> 丰水期预测结果在所考察参数范围内具有稳健性。

Delete repetitive defensive wording such as “不应理解为……”, “并不意味着……”, and repeated “仍需进一步……”. These are editing cues, not forbidden substrings: retain a concise limitation when it changes the decision. Fold it into the relevant method or conclusion instead of adding a disclaimer paragraph. Do not conceal adverse evidence or imply that proposed measures have already worked.

Avoid absolute promises such as “无影响”, “确保达标”, and “风险已消除” unless the claim is actually established for its stated scope. Name the demonstrated outcome, rather than substituting equally vague assurances such as “总体可控” or “具有条件”.

## 5. Be Concrete About Data

Keep the following information traceable in the appropriate table, figure, caption, methods, or text; repeat in analytical prose only what is needed for the judgement:

- sample size and monitoring frequency;
- mean, median, maximum, minimum, range, or percentile;
- year/month/season and statistical period;
- location, section, receptor, or management unit;
- standard name and class limit;
- model scenario, input condition, and uncertainty boundary.

Avoid:

> Water quality has improved in recent years.

Prefer:

> 2021—2024年，主要控制断面COD年均浓度总体下降，部分雨季月份仍出现短时高值。

该示例仅描述趋势与波动。判断是否超标还须核对适用限值及评价统计口径；提出雨季管控要求须有相应污染来源或机制证据，不能仅由短时高值推导。监测用于识别和核查变化，本身不构成改善水质的措施。

Avoid unsupported value-to-judgement jumps:

> The biomass increased to 1.621 mg/L, indicating a low impact.

Prefer:

> The modelled biomass increase is concentrated near the inlet, while the lake-center and outlet changes are smaller. The inlet area is therefore the priority receptor for the bloom-risk assessment.

Use such a conclusion only when the spatial results support it. If the section asks why the increase is localized, add the evidenced mechanism; a spatial pattern alone does not establish its cause. Do not list every potentially relevant factor as a substitute for explaining the result.

## 6. Write Measures as Executable Requirements

Measures should specify as many of the following as the material allows:

- object;
- measure;
- timing or frequency;
- trigger condition;
- responsible direction;
- required record or output;
- how monitoring results will be used.
- effectiveness verification and the applicable recovery or closure condition.

Connect the problem, trigger, action, responsibility, and verification in the owning measures section. Monitoring alone is not a corrective action. Use supplied operating criteria and authority; identify missing material requirements rather than inventing thresholds or responsible organizations. Keep these details out of other chapters except for a necessary concise cross-reference.

Weak:

> Strengthen monitoring management.

Preferred:

> During the initial operation period, conduct follow-up monitoring at the downstream near-field area, downstream control section and sensitive receptors. Focus on COD, ammonia nitrogen, total phosphorus and flow changes. Monitoring results should support operation scheduling, abnormal-condition response and subsequent assessment.
